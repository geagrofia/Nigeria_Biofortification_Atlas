Nigeria Micro-nutrient Deficiencies

Introduction

Nigeria micro-nutrient deficiency data sources

There are number of sources of food consumption and micro-nutrient deficiency data. The three main sources identified for Nigeria have different dates, and differing number of deficiencies and populations ( Table 1 ).

(Federal Government of Nigeria (FGoN) and International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) 2024) (Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare of Nigeria (FMoHSW) et al. 2025) (Ishida et al. 2026 (submitted))

The data at state level need to be joined to the Nigeria States spatial dataset, and the data at zone level need to be joined to the geopolitical zones.

The stages are:

  1. Load the States dataset
  2. Load the geopolitical zone
  3. Load each health database
  4. For each indicator in the database join to the state or zone spatial dataset and plot

Age standardised DALYs Dietary iron Deficiency (IHME, 2023)

Dietary iron deficiency is a measure that seeks to isolate inadequate nutritional intake from other clinical causes of iron deficiency (like blood loss or parasites).

NoteCalculating the dietary iron deficiency

The IHME framework relies on a specialised, multi-step statistical workflow called the GBD Anaemia Causal Attribution framework to calculate dietary iron deficiency (Safiri et al. 2024).

Step 1: Modeling Total Anaemia Prevalence

Before estimating the dietary deficiency itself, IHME models the total envelope of anaemia across the globe (Young et al. 2023).

  • Data Input: IHME extracts individual-level haemoglobin concentration data from major surveys (such as Demographic and Health Surveys) and international databases like the WHO Micronutrient Database.
  • ST-GPR Modeling: They run a statistical engine called Spatiotemporal Gaussian Process Regression (ST-GPR). This mathematical model blends sparse local data with predictive covariates (like the Socio-demographic Index, or SDI) to estimate mean haemoglobin distribution profiles across different age, sex, geography, and time cohorts (Young et al. 2023).
  • Severity Thresholds: They map these curves against clinical cutoffs to divide the population into categories: mild, moderate, or severe anaemia (Safiri et al. 2024).

Step 2: The Counterfactual and Residual Attribution

The core mathematical distinction of IHME’s calculation is that it treats dietary iron deficiency as a residual cause (Young et al. 2023; Safiri et al. 2024). \[{Dietary\ Iron\ Deficiency\ Burden}=\text{Total\ Anemia\ Envelope}-\sum (\text{Explicitly\ Attributed\ Causes})\]

  • Explicit Cause Modelling: The model first subtracts the exact proportion of anaemia cases linked directly to specific conditions modelled within the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) framework. These explicit causes include
    • Infectious diseases (e.g., malaria, HIV/AIDS)
    • Neglected tropical diseases (e.g., hookworm, schistosomiasis)
    • Haemoglobinopathies (e.g., sickle cell disorders, thalassemias)
    • Chronic conditions (e.g., chronic kidney disease, endocrine disorders)
  • The “Residual” Mathematics: Any remaining anaemia cases that cannot be mathematically explained by these specific infections or genetic traits are automatically distributed into the residual pool. Under this framework, dietary iron deficiency is designated as the primary bucket for this remaining health gap (Safiri et al. 2024).

Step 3: Quantifying the Health Loss (YLDs and DALYs)

Once the proportion of anaemia attributed exclusively to dietary iron deficiency is isolated, IHME converts it into standard health metrics:

  1. Calculate YLDs (Years Lived with Disability):

\[{YLD}=\text{Prevalence}\times \text{Disability\ Weight\ (DW)}\] The model uses distinct disability weights evaluated by global panels to reflect the severity of cognitive and physical impairment:

  1. Mild Anaemia DW: 0.004
  2. Moderate Anaemia DW: 0.052
  3. Severe Anaemia DW: 0.149
  1. Calculate DALYs (Disability-Adjusted Life Years): Because dietary iron deficiency rarely causes direct mortality on its own, its DALY total is heavily weighted by YLDs (Huang et al. 2025).

Important Methodological Discrepancy

Because IHME calculates dietary iron deficiency mathematically as a residual framework of anaemia, its final estimations often yield higher prevalence numbers than traditional localized nutrition surveys. Nutrition surveys typically measure objective biomarkers directly (like serum ferritin or soluble transferrin receptor levels), whereas the GBD model absorbs undefined structural anaemias directly into the dietary iron category (Young et al. 2023; Safiri et al. 2024).

Combined Moderate and Severe anaemia when haemoglobin level <11.0 g/dl

The global Biofortification Priority Index (BPI) (Asare-Marfo et al. 2013) uses an indicator of the proportion of preschool-age children with Hb < 110 g/l. This value is equivalent to the definitions of moderate and severe anaemia in children in the NDHS report.

As a result, the two percentages are added together. Due to the low levels of severe anaemia in children the map of combined anaemia is similar to that of moderate anaemia, with high values in Ebonyi, Akwa Ibom and Zamfara.
