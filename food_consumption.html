Compare data sources

To help select the best data source it is necessary to compare the statistics for each indicator from the two sources. However, the indicators in the National Food Consumption and Micronutrient Survey are in many cases different to the GHS survey. The indicators that are most similar are the values for dietary diversity.

Women’s Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD-W)

MDD-W is an indicator that measures the proportion of women 15-49 years of age who consumed food items (at least 15g) from at least five out of the ten defined food groups the previous day or night. The higher the MDD-W, the higher the probability of nutrient adequacy for most key micro-nutrients needed for sustenance of health.

There are two indicators in the datasets for MDD-W: 1) the percentage of women achieving the MDD 2) the average MDD score

Conclusion

The NFCMS data from 2021 show that overall most women do not achieve minimum dietary diversity. The zone with the highest percentage of women achieving MDD is South West. This is the only zone where a majority of women achieve MDD. The zone with the lowest percentage is North West, with only 13.3% of women achieving MDD.

The average MDD scores per zone range between 3 and 4.6. These are low scores, with the southern zones having the most diverse diets. North West zone has the least diverse diet. This indicator is consistent with the % of women achieving MDD.

The percentage of women achieving MDD from the 2024 GHS data show far higher percentages compared to the NFCMS data from 2021, especially in North West and North East zones. However, the South West was no longer the zone with the highest percentage, instead South East zone was the highest with 53.7%.

The pattern from the 2024 GHS data show relatively higher scores for all zones compared to 2021 NFCMS, although the range is still between 3.6 and 4.8. The zone with the lowest diversity is North Central.

Both datasets are from reliable sources, but the GHS data are more recent.

The contribution of plant proteins to energy intakes is far lower than for carbohydrates. The figures do not vary too greatly among the zones, but are consistent with the spatial pattern of the intake. The zone with the lowest contribution is South South with plant proteins contributing to just 5.3% of usual energy intake.

There is no direct comparison in the GHS dataset, but the group of food products most often associated with plant protein is pulses. The proportion of women consuming pulses in the GHS shows a similar pattern to the amount of plant protein consumed in 2021, with women in North East and North West zones the most regular consumers.

Conclusion

Animal proteins are less relevant for crop bio fortification, but they are useful to consider because they are often nutrient dense, an alternative to plant based food products (especially pulses), and many consumers aspire to consume more of these products. The consumption of these products therefore needs to be monitored to see if the Nigerian diet is evolving over time to include more animal products.

Animal protein intake in the 2021 NFCMS is in general lower than plant protein, but there are regional differences. Animal protein intake in South South is almost as high as plant protein intake, whereas in North East the animal protein intake is only 1/4 of the plant protein intake.

The contribution of animal protein to overall usual energy intake follows the same pattern with the South South and South West zones showing the highest contribution.

Women in the North West are the most common consumers of dairy products, while the neighbouring zone of North Central has the lowest percentage of women consuming milk or milk products.

A majority of women consume meat, poultry or fish in the southern zones, while the North West zone has the lowest proportion - perhaps dairy products are a more sustainable source of products for extensive livestock owners.

Eggs have very low consumption throughout Nigeria. The only two zones where these are consumed by more than 15% of women are in South West and South South.

In general the data from the two surveys are complementary and display the same spatial patterns; the NFCMS shows the overall contribution of animal protein to women’s diets, while the GHS shows the prevalence of consumption of animal food products.

Conclusion

Many different food products contribute to micro nutrient intake. The NFCMS highlights the three crops biofortified with Pro-Vitamin A: yellow cassava, orange flesh sweet potato and yellow maize.

The section above shows the proportion of women in the GHS data from 2023/24 consuming non-staple food products, but which are often good sources of micro nutrients.

These maps bolster the message from the minimum dietary diversify section above that show a lack of diversity of food groups, especially in the northern zones.

NoteRelevant Datasets in the GHS

There are five GHS datasets that are relevant for the theme of food consumption:

sect6b_plantingw5.csv
Data collected through Post-Planting Household Questionnaire Section 6B (Food Consumption). This section records household-food item level information on the quantity and value of food consumed within the household during the past 7 days, as well as the quantity and value of food purchased during the past 30 days.

sect6c_plantingw5.csv
Data collected through Post-Planting Household Questionnaire Section 6C (Aggregate Food Consumption). This section records household-food item level information on the number of days the household consumed each food group item during the last 7 days.

sect5b_harvestw5.csv
Data collected through Post- Harvest Household Questionnaire Section 5B (Food Consumption). This section records household-food item level information on the quantity and value of food consumed within the household during the past 7 days, as well as the quantity and value of food purchased during the past 30 days.

sect5c_harvestw5.csv
Data collected through Post-Harvest Household Questionnaire Section 5C (Aggregate Food Consumption). This section records household-food item level information on the number of days the household consumed each food group item during the last 7 days.

sect3d_harvestw5.csv
Data collected through Post-Harvest Household Questionnaire Section 3D (Women Dietary Diversity). This section records women-level information on women’s nutrition diet for the previous day.

This section records household-food item level information on the quantity and value of food consumed within the household during the past 7 days, as well as the quantity and value of food purchased during the past 30 days.

The data unit for these data are food products per household. The response for each food product is either “1. Yes” or “2. No”.

There are over 100 different food products that can be recorded, consequently there are over half a million records for the post-planting dataset.

To map each indicator the data will need to be filtered on the specific indicator, then recoded to a numeric response:

Before After
  1. Yes
 1
  1. No
 0

Once recoded, the statistics for each zone can be computed. The mean value for each zone will show the percentage of households that consumed the particular product.

When the summary statistics are calculated the resulting table can be joined to the zone spatial dataset.
